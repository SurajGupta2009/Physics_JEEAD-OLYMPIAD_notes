# Rutherford, the Bohr Model & Atomic Spectra — first principles to Olympiad

> [!note] Part 24 of [plan.md](../plan.md) · text-only Markdown chapter · written for Obsidian reading mode

**Scope.** The atom as a hypothesis test: Thomson's prediction versus the back-scattered alpha;
Rutherford scattering with the impact-parameter relation and the $\sin^{-4}$ law; the classical
collapse derived with a number; Bohr's postulates reframed as standing-wave consequences; the four
hydrogenic results derived; the Rydberg formula and the five series; photon versus electron
excitation with Franck-Hertz; reduced-mass physics (deuterium, muonic hydrogen, positronium);
Moseley's law as a corollary (derivation owned by PART 25); Zeeman splitting, the quantum numbers
and the correspondence principle; the model's uses today.

**Prerequisites.** PART 23 (standing-wave quantisation, uncertainty estimate, photon bookkeeping);
the Coulomb force and circular-orbit mechanics of the shipped mechanics notes.

**The one idea.** Atoms have discrete levels because an electron is a standing wave, not a planet.

**Contents.** Part 0 orientation and coverage map; 1 intuition; 2 definitions; 3 core derivations
(§3.1-3.12); 4 results ledger and concept checks; 5 exemplars E1-E12; 6 sixteen archetypes and
Q1-Q28; 7 toolkit; 8 eleven traps; 9 playbook; 10 Olympiad block OL1-OL12 (full cross-section
derivation, Bohr-without-postulates, collapse, correspondence expansion, exotic atoms, stellar
Balmer temperature, Rydberg extraction, 21 cm line, helium's two spectra, spectrometer limits, the
energy where Rutherford must fail); 11 the 36-question 200-mark paper; 12 marking and audit;
13 formula sheet; 14 checkpoint and hand-off.

**Coverage (Cengage floor).** *Cengage Optics and Modern Physics* ch 4 Atomic Physics,
pp. 4.1-4.24; every heading of the chapter's contents page (Thomson model, Bohr model with radius,
velocity, orbital frequency, energy, emitted-radiation frequency, hypothetical levels,
hydrogen-like atoms, ionisation and excitation energies and potentials, binding energy, atomic
excitation, limitations, de-excitation wavelengths, hydrogen spectrum, emission and absorption
spectra, nucleus-motion effects, atomic collisions, solved examples, exercise bands) has a row in
the block-0 coverage map. The X-ray half of the same chapter (pp. 4.25-4.32) is PART 25's.
Rutherford scattering, Zeeman/quantum numbers, Franck-Hertz, correspondence and the 21 cm line are
added by the sweep and marked as such.

**Olympiad layer.** Three first-principles derivations (differential cross-section, correspondence
expansion, collapse time), four estimates (stellar Balmer temperature, spectrometer velocity
limit, the energy where Coulomb fails, line-count and shift orders), two measurement
reconstructions (Rydberg from a line list, the H-D doublet), and an explicit limits-and-failure
section.

**Beyond the plan.** Mirrored in `topics.json` `beyond_plan`: Rutherford cross-section as a full
derivation; helium singlet/triplet doorway; 21 cm order of magnitude; spectrometer/Doppler
practical problem; stellar-temperature Boltzmann window as a worked estimate.

**Hand-off.** PART 25 inherits the Bohr arithmetic for inner shells (Moseley derived there), the
Duane-Hunt limit as the inverse photoelectric effect, and Bragg diffraction from PART 23's wave
logic. PART 26 inherits the $r_{\min}$ failure point and the eV bookkeeping. Deliberately skipped:
Schrödinger-equation derivations of the quantum numbers (named, not derived), line intensities,
full fine-structure theory.
