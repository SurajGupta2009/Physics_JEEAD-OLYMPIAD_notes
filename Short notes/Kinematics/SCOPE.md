# Kinematics — scope and classification

This study set combines the repository's consecutive mechanics topics, PART 3 and PART 4: 1-D kinematics and 2-D motion. Their detailed chapters are reference material, not a binding order or content ceiling. The notes use the learning spine **coordinates and signs → graphs and acceleration laws → free fall → relative motion → projectiles → circular kinematics → extensions**.

Classification is by syllabus relevance and prerequisite, not difficulty. Difficult constant-acceleration, graph, projectile, vector, and curvature applications remain in Part I. Part II contains additional physical models or longer methods; it does not mean these ideas can never appear in JEE.

## Part I — JEE Main + Advanced (26 sheets)

- **JEE-01–04 — language of motion:** reference frames; position, displacement and path length; average/instantaneous velocity; speed; acceleration signs; x-t, v-t and a-t slopes/areas.
- **JEE-05–09 — acceleration laws:** derive constant-a equations; free fall; nth-interval displacement versus distance; integrate a(t), separate a(v), use `a = v dv/dx` for a(x), and track branches/turning points.
- **JEE-10–11 — one-dimensional applications:** signed separation and closing; relative acceleration; catch-up; piecewise state ledger; reaction and braking distances.
- **JEE-12–19 — projectile motion:** independent components; horizontal launch; equal-height flight time, height and range; trajectory and velocity; complementary launch angles; unequal elevations; inclined-plane coordinates and optimum; safety envelope with domain conditions.
- **JEE-20–22 — vector relative motion:** vector subtraction; rate of separation; rain/observer; river drift and crossing time; wind and aircraft headings with explicit component signs.
- **JEE-23–25 — angular/circular geometry:** radians, period/frequency, `v = Rω`; polar-basis acceleration derivation; tangential and normal components; curvature radius on a general smooth path.
- **JEE-26 — method selection and validity audit:** choose a route from the given law, check roots, frames, signs, dimensions, limits and contact events.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions (10 sheets)

- **OLY-01** independent-variable choice, variable acceleration and turning branches.
- **OLY-02–03** linear and quadratic drag: exact vertical solutions, terminal states, limits and model regimes.
- **OLY-04** reaction delay plus constant-deceleration braking and scaling.
- **OLY-05** linear-drag projectile components; range at the finite landing event, not an infinite-time integral.
- **OLY-06** constant horizontal deceleration as a deliberately simplified headwind model, including possible horizontal reversal.
- **OLY-07** moving-target interception with gravity by common-time position equality and root filtering.
- **OLY-08** first-order Coriolis estimate for northward motion with local-frame and short-time assumptions.
- **OLY-09** planar pursuit with explicit initial geometry, line-of-sight ODE, numerical path illustration and finite-capture bound when pursuer speed exceeds target speed.
- **OLY-10** Newtonian, vacuum, smooth-path and contact-event limits.

The original chapters include additional problem banks, but this edition is a set of teaching notes rather than a reproduction of every exercise. Content is added or reorganised when it improves correctness or clarity; specific source disagreements are documented in `SOURCE-AUDIT.md`.

## Deliberate boundaries

- Kinematics prescribes acceleration; deriving it from forces belongs to dynamics.
- The equal-height projectile formulas assume uniform gravity, no drag and a flat inertial frame. Other landing levels require the vertical equation and its physical root.
- Drag formulas are model-specific and depend on relative airspeed and flow regime.
- Circular acceleration here is kinematics; identifying the inward force belongs to Newton's laws.
- Full rotating-frame dynamics, non-uniform gravity, relativistic trajectories and 3-D pursuit are beyond this edition.
