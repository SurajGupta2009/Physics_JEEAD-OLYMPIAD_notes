# Scope — relevance, not difficulty

Part I is the full JEE-oriented electrostatics revision spine: hard applications are retained rather than reclassified just for being difficult. Part II collects specialised boundary-value, stability and interaction tools. This is not an official current-syllabus or past-paper frequency audit. An extension may be examinable when its needed ingredients are supplied.

Finite rods, angular density, ring maxima/SHM, full charge equilibrium, cube boundary flux, insulating cavities, particle mechanics, conductor induction, concentric earthing, sphere sharing, pair/self-energy and nuclear-scale checks stay in Part I. They require careful reasoning but grow from the core field/potential/energy framework. Mechanical constraints and nuclear estimates overlap other JEE chapters and are labelled as applications, not exclusive thermodynamics-style or syllabus-frequency claims. The brief Poisson/Laplace consistency bridge in Part I explains derivatives and density; formal harmonic maximum-principle/Hessian machinery is concentrated in Part II.

## Part II: 8 specialised sheets

- **01 Earnshaw and the ring saddle** — Rigorous harmonic maximum principle, Hessian trace and transverse growth clarify why unconstrained static trapping fails; simple constrained stability is already core.
- **02 Images I: a grounded conducting plane** — Grounded-plane image construction, uniqueness, induced density and the energy half-factor require a boundary-value method beyond direct Coulomb/Gauss integration.
- **03 Images II: spherical conductor boundaries** — Grounded and isolated sphere images are specialised geometry-dependent boundary constructions, expanding the detailed source's pointer.
- **04 Pressure, hemispheres and charged bubbles** — Core local conductor stress stays in Part I; hemisphere force, two-surface bubble pressure and dynamical qualifications extend it.
- **05 Drops: shape instability and coalescence** — Rayleigh shape instability and far-separated coalescence endpoint energy require material/shape assumptions not inherent in ordinary charge-system problems.
- **06 Multipoles and induced neutral bodies** — Multipole expansion, quadrupoles and polarisation assembly cost are specialised tools; the ordinary permanent dipole remains core.
- **07 Off-axis rings, cube scaling and numerical checks** — Off-axis elliptic ring potential and singularity-aware cube quadrature are mathematical extensions; symmetry/scaling remain broadly useful methods.
- **08 Inverse-square tests and model boundaries** — Exponent perturbations, null-test interpretation, high-voltage scaling and microscopic/nuclear model limits need explicit experimental/model context.

## Deliberate boundaries

The separately shipped Capacitors chapter covers dielectric networks, capacitance combinations, RC switching and detailed voltage-source energy accounting. This set retains only the necessary capacitance bridge for sphere sharing and emphasises when external-source work would be required. Dynamic electrodynamics, magnetic induction, radiation reaction, quantum material structure and nuclear binding theory are not claimed to be fully taught here.

The 37 sheets consolidate all core source topic families and useful derivations, not every duplicate exercise, all numerical paper solutions or every historical anecdote. Infinite-source, ideal-point, thin-wire, breakdown and material assumptions are visible. Source corrections and unresolved/model-dependent experimental interpretations are in [SOURCE-AUDIT.md](SOURCE-AUDIT.md).
