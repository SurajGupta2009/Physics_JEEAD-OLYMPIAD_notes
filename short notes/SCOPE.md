# Why the capacitor notes are split this way

## The rule: relevance, not difficulty

Part I is a complete, chapter-focused JEE study sequence. It includes useful ways of reasoning and harder applications of core concepts; it is **not** an “easy questions only” section. Part II contains specialised theory and applications that should not interrupt revision of the capacitor chapter for JEE.

This is a pedagogical grouping, **not an official syllabus quotation or a prediction that a particular idea can never appear in an exam**. Syllabuses and question contexts can change. Deriving an unfamiliar result from supplied information is different from needing to memorise a specialised model.

In particular, basic AC and capacitor impedance are relevant to JEE's AC chapter. The former page 12 is placed in the extension section because its **filter transfer functions, compensated probes, threshold oscillators, slow charging and measurement applications** go beyond the capacitor-chapter core requested here—not because all alternating-current physics is outside JEE.

## Part I — JEE Mains + Advanced: 11 sheets

| Local page | Topic | Why it remains here |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Charge, fields, conductors, shielding | Foundation for potentials, induced surface charge and capacitance. |
| 02 | Capacitance, parallel plates, floating metal slab | Definition, geometry and conductor boundaries. |
| 03 | Spheres, coaxial geometry, connected distant spheres | Applications of Gauss, potential integration and equipotential conductors. Results should be derived, not treated as a new memorisation syllabus. |
| 04 | Energy, fixed Q versus fixed V, battery work | Essential correct energy accounting, even in difficult problems. |
| 05 | Plate force, insertion, liquid rise, self-energy | Useful energy/pressure applications; difficulty alone is not a reason to remove them. |
| 06 | Series/parallel, voltage ratings, floating-node charge | Core network reasoning and charge conservation. |
| 07 | Bridges, cube, delta-star, infinite ladders | Useful problem-solving tools derived from the same core laws. Not every special network value needs memorising. |
| 08 | Polarisation, boundaries, layers, patches | Explains dielectric capacitance and how orientation changes the result. D is introduced as a helpful free-charge bookkeeping tool. |
| 09 | Ordinary single-mode RC response | DC final states, charging/discharging and Thevenin reduction. |
| 10 | Charge sharing, heat and branch charge | Conserved island charge, energy loss and charge through a cell. No eigenmode formalism. |
| 11 | JEE checklist and dielectric ratings | Completes the JEE reading sequence with local-field limits, checks and a rapid drill. |

Connected-sphere reasoning replaces exact two-wire hyperbolic formulas on JEE 03. Dielectric strength/voltage ratings have been brought forward from the materials page into JEE 11. Branch charge is kept in JEE 10; matrix-based multi-mode dynamics is not mixed into it.

## Part II — Olympiad / extensions: 6 sheets

“Extension” includes specialised applications and enrichment, not a claim that each item is required by every Olympiad syllabus.

| Local page | Topic | Why it is separate |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Microscopic/dispersive dielectrics | Lorentz local field, Clausius–Mossotti, Debye relaxation, loss models, ferroelectric/piezoelectric response and Paschen breakdown. |
| 02 | AC and specialised applications | Filter transfer functions, compensated divider, threshold oscillator, slow ramp charging and thermal-noise context. This is the former page 12. |
| 03 | Coupled RC and specialised models | Matrix relaxation rates, an explicit two-mode example, meter loading/noise, exact wire/ground-plane geometries. The loading example is a bridge from JEE charge sharing to specialised measurement, not a new prerequisite. |
| 04 | Matrices, reciprocity and images | Capacitance coefficients, weighting potentials, grounded-plane and sphere image machinery. Some individual ideas may be useful in JEE; the systematic toolkit is kept optional here. |
| 05 | Shape, conformal and distributed tools | Spheroids, ellipsoidal inclusions, wedges/corner singularities and porous-electrode diffusion. This is the former page 14. |
| 06 | Instabilities | MEMS pull-in and soft modes, constraint-dependent stability and the Rayleigh drop limit. Basic force/energy reasoning remains in the JEE part. |
