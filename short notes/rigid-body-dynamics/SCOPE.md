# Scope and coverage — dense JEE revision

## What changed

The JEE part is now **19 sheets**, followed by **4 separate specialised extension sheets**. The previous 14-sheet JEE selection has been replaced in place; there is no competing second edition. Coverage, not a target page count, determines pagination.

This revision explicitly combines **theory + equations to learn + short derivations + physical ideas + assumptions and traps**. It is not a formula-only handout. The four MOI sheets are a direct response to the need for a complete, useful JEE recall-and-derivation toolkit.

## What to know directly versus derive

**Know and practise recalling:** I=sum(m r-perp²), radius of gyration, rod centre/end, rectangle/square axes, ring/disc/annulus axes, cylinder axial/transverse, solid sphere and thin spherical-shell diameters, parallel/perpendicular-axis theorems; torque, angular momentum, KE/work/power; common pure-rolling, slip-to-roll, angular-impulse and physical-pendulum results. Always recall the axis and conditions with the coefficient.

**Derive when needed:** cut-out and composite shapes, inclined rod axes, displaced axes, density profiles, special contact geometries, eccentric impacts and loop constraints. The sheets show the short path rather than demand memorisation of an unlimited catalogue. Thick-shell and cone expressions illustrate the same integration tools; an unfamiliar solid need not be a new theory to memorise.

## Relevance, not difficulty

Part I remains a complete JEE-focused sequence. Slipping, the yo-yo, spool reversal, free-target impact, centre of percussion, curved rolling, pendulum/bowl oscillations and sliding-rod detachment stay here as useful applications of core principles. A result is not moved to Olympiad merely because its derivation is harder or the source labels it OL.

Part II separates systematic 3-D inertia tensors, heavy-top/precession stability theory, elastic-disc stress limits and unresolved carpet/peg models. A supplied material or unfamiliar geometry can still occur as a JEE problem context: this is a study classification, not a claim that it can never appear.

Physical pendulums and bowl oscillations connect to JEE's oscillations chapter. Core flywheel kinetic energy remains in JEE; its specialised elastic stress field is optional. Elementary stress/strain itself belongs to elasticity. This is not an official current-syllabus quotation or exam-frequency prediction.

## Boundaries and validation

- No full exercise paper or marking scheme is reproduced. Repeated warnings are consolidated beside the relevant result; incorrect MCQs are not copied.
- No unqualified vector tau=I alpha for 3-D rotation, universal zero-work assertion for friction on moving supports, or fixed-hinge assumption for an instantaneous centre.
- Carpet deposition and peg capture are still under-specified. Conditional phase logic is explained; no unique unsupported answer is claimed.
- The source chapter itself is unchanged. [SOURCE-AUDIT.md](SOURCE-AUDIT.md) records corrections, assumptions and the added standard-result derivations.
- Reading order: JEE 01–19 in full, then extension 01–04. Every image has its own visible part label/local number. Combined ZIP/PDF order follows the same sequence.
- Render, export and physics test details are in [README.md](README.md) and [verification.json](verification.json). They are representative physics checks, not exhaustive certification of every source exercise.
