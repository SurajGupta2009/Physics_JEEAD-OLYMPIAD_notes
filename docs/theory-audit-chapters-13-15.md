# Theory-only audit: chapters 13–15

**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Scope:** theory/derivation spine only; excludes question banks, worked-solution keys, diagrams, and experimental-practical coverage.

## Bottom line and order

The next three course chapters are **String Waves (13), Sound Waves (14), and Thermodynamics (15)**. Their order makes sense: establish wave propagation and boundary behaviour on a string, apply wave and oscillation ideas to sound in media, then move to the thermal state variables and energy laws used in acoustics and elsewhere. The notes are explanation-heavy and cover the central JEE theory plus substantial NSEP/INPhO extensions.

The core theory is broad, but this targeted review does **not** certify every equation in these long notes or the full JEE syllabus. Several advanced sections require careful assumptions, and the practical/experimental-skills syllabus was not assessed.

## Findings and theory edits

### Chapter 13 — String waves

The progression from travelling profiles and the string wave equation to particle motion, energy transport, reflection/transmission, standing waves, and normal modes is sound. The small-slope, uniform-tension assumptions are important and mostly visible.

- Clarified the definition of waves: no sustained bulk matter transport is the usual linear-wave picture, not a universal statement for nonlinear waves.
- Removed an invalid “must be bounded everywhere” test for mathematical wave solutions. Boundedness/finite energy is a physical requirement for a particular setup, not a condition for solving the wave equation.
- Clarified that zero horizontal force in the small-slope derivation does not itself prove zero longitudinal displacement; longitudinal motion and second-order stretch are neglected in the linear transverse model.

### Chapter 14 — Sound waves

The core order—longitudinal displacement and pressure → sound speed → intensity → boundary modes → interference/beats → Doppler—is appropriate. The material clearly distinguishes particle motion from propagation and pressure nodes from displacement nodes.

- Corrected the sign/phase convention for excess pressure for the declared right-travelling sine wave: pressure is $-\Delta P_0\cos(kx-\omega t+\phi)$, lags displacement by a quarter-cycle under this convention, and is in phase with particle velocity. The pressure-amplitude magnitude and intensity results remain positive and unchanged.
- Removed an inconsistent scratch expression for energy density and stated the pointwise equipartition/instantaneous total density cleanly for the ideal progressive harmonic wave.
- Rewrote the wind and oblique-Doppler passages with a single signed line-of-sight formula, explicit source/observer projections, and a stated wind convention; removed unresolved sign questions.
- Replaced scratchwork in the organ-pipe boundary summary with the correct displacement/pressure boundary conditions and effective-length qualification.
- Clarified that the slowly varying duct amplitude law is a WKB/no-strong-reflection approximation and fixed the relation between amplitude and intensity.

### Chapter 15 — Thermodynamics

The conceptual spine is well organised: temperature and measurement → kinetic theory → heat/work/internal energy and the first law → standard processes → engines/refrigerators → entropy and extensions. Sign convention ($\Delta U=Q-W$, work by the gas) is stated. The course distinguishes state variables from energy transfers and emphasizes that adiabatic does not automatically mean reversible.

- Clarified that the kelvin is defined by fixing Boltzmann’s constant; $\tfrac32kT$ per translational degree of freedom is a classical ideal-gas consequence, not the general definition of temperature.
- Clarified molar $C_V$ and $C_P$ definitions and the constant-volume vs constant-pressure measurement conditions.
- Generalized the ideal-gas internal-energy and entropy changes to integrals when heat capacity varies with temperature; the familiar $nC_V\Delta T$ and logarithmic form are now explicitly identified as constant-$C_V$ simplifications.
- Stated that standard constant-heat-capacity process formulas use the usual JEE approximation; changing degrees of freedom/temperature-dependent capacities require integration and can invalidate constant-$\gamma$ power laws.
