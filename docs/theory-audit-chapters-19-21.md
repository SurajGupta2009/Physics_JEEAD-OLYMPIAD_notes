# Theory-only audit: chapters 19–21

**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Scope:** theory/derivation spine only; excludes question banks, worked-solution keys, diagrams, and experimental-practical coverage.

## Bottom line and order

The next three course chapters are **Current electricity (19), Magnetism (20), and Induction, inductance & AC (21)**. Their sequence is effective: establish charge flow and circuit analysis; introduce magnetic forces and fields from steady currents; then bring time-varying fields, induction, inductors, and AC together. This progression also makes useful links across the chapters explicit.

The sampled theory is strongly organized around derivations, sign conventions, and validity conditions. One factual correction was made in Magnetism: since the 2019 SI revision, the ampere is defined by fixing the elementary charge, so $\mu_0$ is measured rather than exactly $4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m A$^{-1}$. The old value remains an excellent JEE approximation. The chapter’s front matter and a later historical example now state this consistently.

## Findings by chapter

### Chapter 19 — Current electricity

The progression from current and drift to resistivity/materials, real sources, Kirchhoff networks, bridges, DC capacitor transients, and network theorems is well motivated. The sampled material distinguishes current from carrier drift, ideal from real sources, and steady state from transient behaviour. Kirchhoff sign conventions and power checks are useful safeguards. The notes also correctly qualify superposition to linear networks and warn that power itself does not superpose.

- The RC discussion gives the one-capacitor Thevenin-resistance method and notes that networks with multiple independent charge variables can have multiple time constants; retain that qualification when applying a single-exponential formula.
- **Scope/validity:** this review sampled the theory sequence and selected results, not every later network theorem or nonlinear-material treatment.

### Chapter 20 — Magnetism

The order—magnetic force on moving charges, particle trajectories and applications, field sources via Biot–Savart/Ampère, forces and torques, then magnetic matter and Earth—is pedagogically sound. Direction conventions and the separation of magnetostatic results from induction are clearly stated. The sampled field and force relations consistently name idealizations such as long solenoids, thin wires, steady currents, and linear media.

- Corrected the SI-history wording around $\mu_0$: the pre-2019 ampere definition fixed its value; under the present SI it is experimentally determined. Numerical formulas retain the familiar rounded value, identified as an approximation.
- **Scope/validity:** the extensive advanced material on magnetised matter, relativistic interpretation, and Earth-field applications was not independently re-derived line by line.

### Chapter 21 — Induction, inductance & AC

The theory follows a coherent arc: flux and Faraday–Lenz law; motional EMF and coupled rod/circuit examples; induced fields and eddy currents; generators/motors; flux linkage and inductance; RL/LC transients; then RMS, phasors, LCR resonance, power, transformers, and rectification. The treatment explicitly separates non-conservative induced electric fields from electrostatic potential differences and develops AC phase relations from element laws.

- The resonance discussion identifies the series-circuit assumptions and distinguishes series from parallel resonance. Phasor addition, rather than scalar addition of AC voltage magnitudes, is emphasized correctly.
- Ideal transformer, sinusoidal steady-state, lumped-element, and linear-inductance formulas require their stated assumptions; real losses, parasitics, saturation, and frequency-dependent material response limit them.
- **Scope/validity:** selected derivations and formulas were checked; this was not a full independent reconstruction of every coupled-circuit and advanced induction derivation.
