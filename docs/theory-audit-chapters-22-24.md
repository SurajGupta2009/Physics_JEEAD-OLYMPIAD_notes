# Theory-only audit: chapters 22–24

**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Scope:** theory/derivation spine only; excludes question banks, worked-solution keys, diagrams, and experimental-practical coverage.

## Bottom line and order

The next three chapters are **Electromagnetic waves (22), Geometrical optics (23), and Wave optics (24)**. The order works well: Maxwell’s field laws establish light as an electromagnetic wave; geometrical optics then treats ray propagation and image formation in the appropriate limit; wave optics returns to phase and interference when ray descriptions alone are insufficient. The notes connect these models while stating limits such as paraxial rays, ideal interfaces, and plane-wave assumptions.

No theory edit was made in this review. The sampled derivations did not reveal a confirmed theory error. This is a targeted review rather than a line-by-line certification of all three long chapters.

## Findings by chapter

### Chapter 22 — Electromagnetic waves

The derivation sequence—from the capacitor/Ampère-law consistency issue and displacement current, through the Maxwell field laws and source-free wave equations, to plane waves, energy, momentum, and radiation pressure—is logically ordered. The manuscript distinguishes vacuum laws from macroscopic material descriptions and states conditions for the wave equation and Poynting results. In the sampled sections, the displacement-current bookkeeping, constitutive assumptions, wave speed, travelling-wave energy flux, and momentum discussion were internally consistent.

- The wave-equation derivation correctly requires a homogeneous, source-free, linear medium with constant constitutive parameters; in matter with spatially varying or dispersive parameters, the simple scalar wave equation and $n=\sqrt{\mu_r\varepsilon_r}$ require qualification.
- The energy-density and intensity formulas are tied to the stated travelling-wave/linear-medium assumptions, rather than presented as universal for arbitrary fields.
- **Scope/validity:** radiation pressure on curved surfaces, material momentum, and advanced impedance/boundary cases were not independently re-derived in full.

### Chapter 23 — Geometrical optics

The progression from rays and plane mirrors through spherical mirrors, refraction and total internal reflection, prisms/dispersion, lenses and systems, and optical instruments is appropriate. The notes establish one Cartesian sign convention and emphasize checking the physical meaning of signed image positions. They also distinguish paraxial formulas from exact/non-paraxial behaviour in the advanced layer. The sampled mirror formulas, refraction convention, and paraxial qualifications were consistent.

- The standard mirror, spherical-surface, and thin-lens relations require their stated paraxial/small-angle assumptions; ray tracing outside that regime can produce aberrations rather than the ideal image predicted by first-order formulas.
- **Scope/validity:** not every instrument, thick-lens, aberration, and exact-ray derivation was independently checked. The audit excludes diagrams and problem-solution keys by request.

### Chapter 24 — Wave optics

The chapter builds from wavefronts and Huygens’ principle to superposition/coherence, YDSE, films and Newton’s rings, diffraction/gratings/resolution, and polarisation. This is a strong conceptual order: phase and optical path are introduced before applying them to interference, and single-slit envelopes and source coherence are brought back when the ideal two-point-source model needs refinement. Sampled equations for phase/path difference, YDSE intensity, visibility, slab-induced shift, and oblique incidence were consistent with the stated approximations.

- The familiar fringe-width and screen-position formulas use small-angle/far-screen geometry; exact angular conditions are retained where the chapter discusses order limits.
- Thin-film results depend on refractive-index ordering and the corresponding phase reversal on reflection; the notes explicitly direct attention to those conditions.
- **Scope/validity:** the full diffraction, grating, resolution, polarisation, and coherence sections were not re-derived line by line.
