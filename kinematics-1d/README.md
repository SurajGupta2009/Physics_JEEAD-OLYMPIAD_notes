# Kinematics in One Dimension — first principles to Olympiad

> [!note] Part 3 of [plan.md](../plan.md) · text-only Markdown chapter · written for Obsidian reading mode

**Scope.** Frame of reference, position, distance vs displacement, average and instantaneous velocity and acceleration, the $x$–$t$, $v$–$t$, $a$–$t$ graph trio, constant-acceleration equations derived by integration, free fall, variable acceleration ($a=f(t)$, $a=f(v)$, $a=f(x)$), relative motion in 1-D, and piecewise kinematics.

**Prerequisites.** PART 1 (units, dimensions, error propagation) and PART 2 (vectors, components, the concept of direction).

**The one idea.** Motion is one function $x(t)$; everything else in kinematics is its slope or its area.

**Contents.** Part 0 orientation; 1 intuition; 2 definitions; 3 core derivations (§3.1–3.12); 4 results ledger; 5 exemplars E1–E10; 6 archetypes and Q1–Q25; 7 toolkit; 8 traps; 9 playbook; 10 Olympiad block OL1–OL10; 11 paper; 12 marking; 13 formula sheet; 14 checkpoint.

**Coverage (Cengage floor).** *Cengage Mechanics I*, ch 4 Motion in One Dimension (position, displacement, velocity, acceleration, graphs, constant-acceleration equations, free fall, variable acceleration, relative motion).

**Olympiad layer.** Linear drag $v(t)=v_0e^{-kt}$ and quadratic drag solutions, terminal speed, the exponential–hyperbolic contrast, the pursuit problem, the $v\,dv/dx$ method for distance-dependent forces, the "does a heavier body fall faster with drag?" analysis, drag-coefficient estimation, the reaction-time stopping-distance design problem, relativistic 1-D kinematics as a limit-preview (link PART 28).
